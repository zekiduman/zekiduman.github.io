01 / About

Engineering rigor, applied to the living body.

My PhD sits between control engineering and pharmacology. Co-advised by Prof. João P. Hespanha (ECE) and Prof. Kevin W. Plaxco (Chemistry) at UCSB's Institute for Collaborative Biotechnologies, I work on a simple-sounding question: if we can measure a drug's concentration in the body every few seconds, can we dose it precisely enough to hold that concentration exactly where we want it?

Answering that has taken the full pipeline: signal processing to clean up raw electrochemical sensor data, compartmental PK models and parameter estimation to describe how drugs move between plasma and tissue, and model predictive and adaptive control to make dosing decisions in real time during in-vivo experiments. Along the way I've worked closely with chemists and life scientists to design experiments and translate results into decisions.

I'm finishing my PhD in December 2026 and looking for roles in pharmacometrics / quantitative pharmacology, model-informed drug development, and data science or modeling positions where time-series data, optimization and dynamical models meet real decisions.

02 / Research

From raw signal to dosing decision.

Signal processing for real-time drug monitoring

Electrochemical aptamer-based (EAB) sensors report drug levels every few seconds, but the raw voltammograms are noisy. I develop fitting and peak-extraction methods that make those measurements precise enough to act on.

  • Multi-Gaussian with Laplacian voltammogram fitting
  • Peak extraction for square-wave voltammetry
  • Published in ACS Sensors, 2025

Pharmacokinetic modeling & system identification

I design compartmental PK models for drugs and metabolites in plasma and solid tissue, and fit them to high-frequency in-vivo data using nonlinear programming.

  • Compartmental & state-space ODE models
  • Parameter estimation with CasADi / IPOPT
  • Plasma-to-tissue transport kinetics

Model-informed closed-loop drug delivery

Using model predictive control with moving horizon estimation, I regulate methotrexate concentrations in real time in rats, and am extending this to population-based adaptive control across subjects.

  • MPC + moving horizon estimation
  • Adaptive control for inter-subject variability
  • ACS Pharmacol. Transl. Sci., 2025
